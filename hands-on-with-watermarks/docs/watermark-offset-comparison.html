Watermarks: earliest-offset vs latest-offset

A Kafka topic of clicks whose event times are out of order by up to 5 s. The query uses WATERMARK FOR event_time AS event_time - INTERVAL '1' SECOND, and a record is late when its event time is at or below the current watermark. The only difference between the two panels is scan.startup.mode.

on time late watermark

earliest-offset replays history

Starts at offset 0 and races through the backlog. Each 200 ms watermark tick consumes roughly ≈200 s of event time, so the watermark is left far behind the data.

Real measured run (35 s): 395,180 events → 1,576 late (0.4%)

Flink emits a watermark only every ~200 ms — not per event. While replaying, the watermark cannot keep up, so practically nothing looks late.

latest-offset at the live head

Starts at the live head and reads only new records. Each 200 ms tick consumes roughly ≈0.8 s of event time, so the watermark tracks the data closely.

At the live head the watermark tracks real time, so events more than 1 s out of order fall below it and are flagged late.

The bounded query you ran: ... (SELECT * FROM ooo_clicks LIMIT 1000) with earliest-offset. The 1000 rows drain in under the first 200 ms tick, before any watermark exists, so late = 0 exactly — and the streaming job never finishes. That is why the exercise says an empty result, not a zero.
The animation is a scaled schematic (a handful of sample points per tick); the counters and the measured figures carry the real magnitudes. The takeaway: lateness is governed by how much event time the reader consumes between watermark ticks — large during backlog replay (so lateness looks like zero), small at the live head (so it becomes visible). Use latest-offset to study lateness; use earliest-offset to backfill, but expect its watermark lag to mask it.
